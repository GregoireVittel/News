How the index is built

  • Readings: every CAC 40 trading day gets up to four raw readings: the CAC 40's distance from its 125-day average; its 20-day volatility (standard deviation of daily log returns, annualised); the 20-day change in EUR/CHF; and the CAC 40's 20-day return minus that of gold priced in euros (gold futures divided by EUR/USD).
  • Scores: each reading is ranked as a percentile within its last 756 trading days (about 3 years), using that day and earlier days only. A rank needs at least 252 days of history. Volatility is flipped (100 minus the rank) so that, for every reading, high means greed.
  • Index: the plain average of the readings available that day, left blank when fewer than two are. Currency and gold prices carry their last value over market holidays for up to 5 days.
  • Bands: below 25 extreme fear, 25–45 fear, 45–55 neutral, 55–75 greed, 75 and above extreme greed.
  • Relative, not absolute: each reading is judged against the last three years, so the same volatility can score as fear after a calm stretch and as calm after a crisis. It describes how stretched the mood is, not a timing signal.
  • Data: daily closes from Yahoo Finance (CAC 40, EUR/CHF, gold futures, EUR/USD), refreshed every hour. Not investment advice.
